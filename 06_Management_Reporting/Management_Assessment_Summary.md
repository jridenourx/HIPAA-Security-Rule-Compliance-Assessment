## 1. Executive Summary

A simulated HIPAA Security Rule compliance assessment was performed for Northstar Health Plan to evaluate selected administrative, physical, and technical safeguards protecting electronic protected health information (ePHI).

The assessment covered 13 selected HIPAA Security Rule controls and reviewed eight synthetic organizational evidence documents, including policies, procedures, and supporting operational records.

The assessment identified opportunities to strengthen Northstar's security program, particularly in risk management, workforce access administration, technical safeguard validation, audit logging, and security monitoring.

**Overall, all 13 assessed controls were rated Partially Effective.**

The assessment identified 15 formal findings:

- **10 High severity findings**
- **5 Moderate severity findings**
- **1 additional observation** related to incident response execution evidence

The most significant concerns involve an outdated security risk analysis, the absence of a formal risk treatment plan, incomplete workforce access management evidence, and insufficient technical evidence demonstrating that safeguards are consistently implemented.

Northstar has documented a foundation of security policies and procedures. However, documentation alone does not establish that safeguards are operating effectively. Additional technical validation, operational records, and management oversight are needed.

A Corrective Action Plan has been developed to assign proposed ownership, establish remediation deadlines, and define evidence requirements for validating completed actions.

## 4. Key Risk Areas

The assessment identified five areas requiring management attention.

### 4.1 Risk Analysis and Risk Management

**Related Controls:** H-01 and H-02  
**Findings:** H-01-01 and H-02-01  
**Priority:** High

The security risk analysis was last updated January 15, 2026 and does not reflect the third-party claims analytics integration introduced in July 2026.

Additionally, the available documentation does not demonstrate a formal risk treatment plan assigning risk owners, remediation actions, and target dates.

**Management Considerations**

- Update the security risk analysis to include material environmental changes.
- Establish a centralized risk treatment register.
- Assign risk owners and remediation deadlines.
- Establish periodic management reporting and risk acceptance procedures.

### 4.2 Workforce Security and Access Management

**Related Controls:** H-03 and H-04  
**Findings:** H-03-01, H-03-02, and H-04-01  
**Priority:** High and Moderate

The assessment identified weaknesses in workforce clearance documentation, workforce access changes, termination processes, and periodic access reviews.

Although E-02 establishes general access management expectations, the available evidence does not demonstrate consistent execution across the workforce and systems containing ePHI.

**Management Considerations**

- Formalize workforce clearance procedures.
- Strengthen HR-to-IT notifications for separations and job changes.
- Establish periodic access reviews.
- Retain evidence of access approvals, modifications, and removals.

### 4.3 Technical Safeguards and Authentication

**Related Controls:** H-09, H-11, H-12, and H-13  
**Findings:** H-09-01, H-09-02, H-11-01, H-12-01, and H-13-01  
**Priority:** High and Moderate

The assessment identified insufficient technical evidence demonstrating that access controls, audit logging, authentication, and transmission security safeguards are consistently configured.

Northstar has documented relevant security expectations, but the assessment did not receive supporting configuration reports, authentication records, audit log samples, or encryption validation evidence.

**Management Considerations**

- Validate unique user identification and session timeout settings.
- Confirm multifactor authentication enforcement for applicable remote access.
- Validate audit logging coverage and retention.
- Obtain encryption and transmission security configuration evidence.
- Formalize emergency access procedures.

### 4.4 Security Awareness and Training

**Related Control:** H-05  
**Findings:** H-05-01 and H-05-02  
**Priority:** High and Moderate

The training evidence identified one employee who completed onboarding training eight days after Northstar's internal 30-day deadline.

The available sample includes 12 employee records from an estimated workforce of 120 and does not demonstrate organization-wide training coverage.

**Management Considerations**

- Maintain a complete workforce training roster.
- Reconcile training assignments and completion against active workforce records.
- Establish overdue training reminders and escalation.
- Retain management review evidence.

### 4.5 Business Continuity and Monitoring

**Related Controls:** H-07, H-08, and H-10  
**Findings:** H-07-01, H-08-01, and H-10-01  
**Priority:** High and Moderate

The assessment identified insufficient backup restoration testing evidence, limited workstation safeguard validation, and incomplete activity review documentation.

E-07 contains four sample monitoring records. Two are marked Complete and two are marked Incomplete. One incomplete record identifies unusual activity but does not include sufficient supporting investigation or follow-up documentation.

**Management Considerations**

- Perform and document backup restoration testing.
- Establish periodic workstation security reviews.
- Approve a formal activity review schedule.
- Document investigation and resolution of monitoring exceptions.

## 7. Additional Observation

### H-06-01 – Incident Response Execution Not Demonstrated

E-04 documents a security incident response procedure, but no incident tickets, exercise records, or other execution evidence were provided.

This is tracked as a separate evidence improvement observation rather than a formal finding.

Management should consider conducting a tabletop incident response exercise and retaining supporting documentation.

The absence of exercise records does not establish that Northstar has failed to respond to actual incidents.

## 8. Overall Conclusion

Based on the synthetic evidence reviewed, Northstar has established a foundation of security policies and procedures addressing selected HIPAA Security Rule requirements.

However, the assessment identified deficiencies in risk analysis, risk management, workforce access administration, training coverage, backup restoration evidence, technical safeguard validation, and security monitoring documentation.

All 13 selected controls were rated Partially Effective.

The 15 formal findings and one additional observation provide management with a structured basis for prioritizing remediation and improving the organization's ability to demonstrate the implementation and operation of security safeguards.

Management should prioritize the high-severity findings, establish clear accountability for remediation, and require evidence-based validation before findings are closed.

This assessment is limited to the selected requirements and synthetic evidence included in the portfolio project. It does not establish whether an actual organization is compliant or noncompliant with the HIPAA Security Rule.
